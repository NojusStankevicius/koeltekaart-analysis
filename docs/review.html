01 · What holds up

Five findings I would defend in a review

§6

Network distance changed the answer, not just the precision

Straight-line distance put 50.9% of residents within 500 m. Routing over the OSM pedestrian graph gives 23.7% — a gap of up to 31 percentage points, median detour ratio 1.55. In a city built on water this is not a refinement; it is the difference between two conclusions.

Guarded by assert near["detour"].min() >= 1, which caught a real bug: facility-side snap distance was missing, producing walking routes shorter than the straight line.

§11 · §12

Two independent exposure measures agree

Green-blue land cover from BGT rises with distance from a cooling place (r = +0.208); dwelling overheating from NTA 8800 TOjuli falls with it (r = −0.120). Land cover and building physics are unrelated data sources reaching the same conclusion.

This is the strongest evidence in the project, and it is the one result I would lead with: the areas furthest from a cooling place are not the areas that most need one.

§9 · §10

Two clean nulls, reported as nulls

Coverage for over-65s is 22.9% against 23.7% for everyone; for people living alone it is 26.1% — slightly better. Neither age nor isolation predicts access. The 80 existing places track population density, because population density is what they track.

§14

The weighting argument was settled by measurement

Ranking candidate sites by unweighted population versus population weighted 5× toward the elderly gives Spearman correlations of 0.997–1.000. The composite-versus-filter debate turned out to be about a decision that does not change the answer.

§9 · §12

Suppression handled as bounds rather than guesses

CBS withholds counts under 5. Rather than impute, every elderly figure was computed twice — all suppressed values at 0, then at 4 — giving 122,365–128,785 over-65s, an interval narrow enough that no conclusion depends on it.

The same technique was then applied to the elderly-living-alone interaction and failed loudly: bounds of 2,920–108,830, a 37-fold range. That analysis was dropped rather than published. Two lines of code prevented a plausible-looking, meaningless result.

02 · What it cannot support

Limits a reviewer would find first

no outcome

Nothing here is validated against actual harm

There is no outcome variable — no heat mortality, no ambulance callouts, no admissions at small-area geography. Every axis is exposure or proxy. The analysis can say who is far from shelter in hot housing; it cannot say that this predicts anyone being harmed. That is the single biggest limitation and no amount of extra data in the current set fixes it.

16% of city

141,000 residents have unmeasurable housing

TOjuli exists only for dwellings labelled since 2021 — 41.4% of stock. Coverage is spatially even (37.7–46.2% by stadsdeel) and the selection effect is 0.02 on the median, both tested. But 2,137 squares fall below five labelled dwellings and are excluded outright. That exclusion also silently flattened the green-cover gradient, because the dropped squares are disproportionately green.

§11

Surface cover cannot see trees

BGT records ground cover, so a paved street under full canopy scores as pure stone — and dense central streets are exactly where Amsterdam's street trees are. The “stoniest quarter is best served” result is biased by an unknown amount in an unknown direction until canopy is added.

§6

Ferries are missing from the walking graph

network_type="walk" excludes them, so the free GVB crossings of the IJ are modelled as impossible. Measured impact is smaller than feared — Noord's median detour is 1.57, in line with Oost — but Weesp at 2.98 and any cross-IJ conclusion remain unreliable.

§7 · §13

The 1 km threshold was chosen for legibility, not for health

At 500 m the underserved area is a single connected blob of 27 km² holding over 400,000 people — true and useless. 1 km produces nameable zones. That is a presentational reason, stated honestly in the notebook, but it is generous for the heat-vulnerable population the analysis is about.

scope

Availability is not modelled at all

Opening hours were excluded deliberately — they change seasonally and belong in an operational tool. The consequence must travel with every figure: real availability during a heatwave is lower than any number here, possibly much lower at weekends. Capacity is likewise ignored; a library and a supermarket café count the same.

03 · Project health

The uncomfortable part

The analysis is in better shape than the project around it. None of the following affects a result; all of it affects whether the results survive contact with next week.

critical

No version control, at all

git log reports no commits on main. Every methodological decision, every bug fix, all fourteen sections exist in exactly one 1.5 MB file with no history. A bad overwrite loses the lot. This was flagged on day one and has been outstanding since.

critical

data/raw/SOURCES.md was never written

Decisions 1 and 2 rest on it: raw inputs are a frozen, immutable snapshot whose provenance lives in SOURCES.md rather than in version control. The file does not exist. 11 GB of inputs — CBS grid, BGT extracts, BAG, EP-Online, the OSM graph — currently have no record of URL, retrieval date or version anywhere except prose scattered through the notebook.

The raw-immutability rule was already broken once: an overwritten locations export is how the original geocoder validation was lost.

moderate

One namespace, 104 cells, two collisions already

gap was overwritten by a float in a figure cell; b — the buurten frame — by a loop variable. Both failures surfaced sections away from their cause. A third is a matter of time; nothing has graduated into src/, which still contains only the generated hello-world.

minor

Environment drifts from what the code imports

requests is imported in §2 but absent from pyproject.toml — it works only because osmnx pulls it in transitively, which is precisely the failure the notebook's own prose warns about. folium is declared and no longer used. README.md is empty; notebooks/ is empty.

minor

Six caches, all invalidated by hand

Every expensive step short-circuits on a file in data/processed/. That is the right design, but changing an upstream filter moves nothing until the right cache is deleted manually. This has already produced one stale run.

If you only do one thing

Commit the work and write SOURCES.md. The analysis is genuinely good — two independent exposure measures agreeing, a weighting question settled empirically, an unmeasurable interaction dropped rather than fudged. All of it currently sits in one uncommitted file beside eleven gigabytes of undocumented inputs. The methodology is more defensible than the thing holding it.
