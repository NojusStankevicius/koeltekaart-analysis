# Koeltekaart Amsterdam — coverage, exposure and vulnerability analysis

Where in Amsterdam are people underserved by the city's ~80 indoor cooling places
(*koelteplekken*), and where does that lack of access coincide with genuine need?

The analysis works at **100 m resolution** using CBS population squares, measures **walking
distance over the OpenStreetMap pedestrian network** rather than straight lines, and
characterises need along two independent axes: **exposure** (vegetation cover from BGT, and
dwelling overheating from NTA 8800 TOjuli) and **vulnerability** (age and household composition
from CBS). Section 15 turns the question around and asks what the existing supply is made of:
how many residents each venue carries, and how many of them have no second option.

## Headline results

- Measured over the street network, **23.7%** of residents are within 500 m of a cooling place —
  not the **50.9%** straight-line distance suggests.
- Access and heat exposure run in **opposite** directions. Two unrelated measures agree *on that
  gradient*: the stoniest, hottest-housed quarter of the city is the best served (28.6% within
  500 m against 16.6% for the greenest). They do **not** agree on which individual square is hot
  (Spearman −0.222) — see decision 19.
- Neither age nor living alone predicts access. The existing places track population density.
- 42 underserved zones hold 331,895 residents; they divide into four distinct kinds of need.
- Coverage is not only thin, it is **single-threaded**: of the 214,600 residents within 500 m of a
  cooling place, **180,685 (84%) can reach exactly one**, and 50 of the 78 venues have no backup at
  all — everyone they serve has nowhere else to walk.

Full reasoning, including every rejected alternative, is in **[`docs/decisions.md`](docs/decisions.md)**
(21 entries). An honest audit of what the analysis can and cannot support is in
[`docs/review.html`](docs/review.html).

## Running it

```bash
uv sync
uv run jupyter lab koeltekaart.ipynb
```

Raw inputs are **not** in this repository — see [`data/raw/SOURCES.md`](data/raw/SOURCES.md) for
what to download and from where (~11 GB; CBS, PDOK BGT, PDOK BAG, EP-Online, OSM). Everything in
`data/processed/` is derived and regenerates from those inputs.

Each expensive step caches to `data/processed/`. **Changing an upstream filter does nothing until
the relevant cache file is deleted** — that has already caused one stale run.

`scipy` is used directly in sections 6e and 15 (`csgraph.dijkstra`, `connected_components`), and
`osmnx.distance.nearest_nodes` needs it to snap points against a projected graph regardless.

## Layout

```
koeltekaart.ipynb     the analysis, sections 1–15, run top to bottom
data/raw/             immutable inputs (gitignored) + SOURCES.md
data/processed/       derived outputs, safe to delete
docs/decisions.md     every methodological choice and what was rejected
docs/figures/         eleven figures, regenerated by the notebook
docs/assignment-brief.pdf  the brief this started from
```

## Status

Exploratory and unpublished. Known limitations — no outcome variable, ferries missing from the
walking graph, tree canopy invisible to the surface-cover proxy, 16% of residents in squares too
sparse to measure housing — are documented rather than fixed. Read `docs/review.html` before
citing any figure.
